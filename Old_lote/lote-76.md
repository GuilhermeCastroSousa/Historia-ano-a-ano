Was the Cyrus Cylinder really the world’s first declaration of human rights, or was it something more calculated? Created after Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon in 539 BCE, this famous inscription presents the Persian king as a liberator chosen by Marduk to rescue the city from Nabonidus. This video examines what the Cylinder actually says, why it became so famous, and how its modern reputation was shaped by both the Shah of Iran and the Islamic Republic. We explore its roots in ancient Mesopotamian royal propaganda, the role of Babylonian priests and local gods, and the political bargain that helped Cyrus secure his new conquest. The story also reveals how Achaemenid religious policy worked in practice. Cyrus did not simply tolerate Babylonian traditions. He embraced and financed them, using local elites, temples, and familiar ideas of kingship to stabilize Persian rule. Finally, we investigate the Cylinder’s disputed connection to the end of the Babylonian Exile, the Hebrew Bible, and Cyrus’s reputation as a messiah. The result is a more complicated portrait of Cyrus the Great: not merely a tolerant visionary, but a brilliant imperial statesman who turned conquest into legitimacy and created one of history’s most enduring political messages today.

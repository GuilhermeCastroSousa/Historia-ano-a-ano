descricao: In this video we'll explore the ruins of Takht-e Soleyman, one of Iran's most remarkable archaeological sites and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, located in the mountains of northwestern Iran. Takht-e Soleyman rose to prominence under the Sasanian Empire (224–651), when it became home to Adur Gushnasp, one of the three great sacred fires of Zoroastrianism. Closely associated with the Sasanian kings, the sanctuary was an important center of religious worship and royal ceremony. At the heart of the complex lies an extraordinary natural lake, reaching a depth of approximately 112 meters (367 feet). Surrounding it are the remains of the great fire temple of Adur Gushnasp, monumental halls, courtyards, and massive fortification walls. Centuries later, around 1270, the Mongol Ilkhanids transformed parts of the former sanctuary into a royal palace, adding residences, arcades, and ceremonial buildings.
